# Emirates flight optimisation criteria

Nine commercial dimensions decide if this opportunity is worth pursuing. Technical weather and fuel rules sit under dimension 4. Plain language. Use before every pilot or claim.

## One line rule

No clear problem cost and no weather pass means no path ranking claim. No written win number means no success claim.

## Opportunity in one sentence

Help Emirates burn less trip fuel on Dubai long haul flights by checking forecast honesty on real flown tracks first, then ranking nearby paths only when weather is trustworthy.

---

## 1. TAM (Total Addressable Market)

**Definition for this opportunity**
All airline spend worldwide on wind aware flight planning, route fuel optimisation, and related aviation weather decision tools that could one day use a forecast verification first approach.

**What we know now**
- Large legacy vendors sell route and flight planning suites to many airlines.
- Fuel is a top airline operating cost, so the category is real and funded.

**What research must fill**
- Credible global market size (USD) for flight planning / fuel optimisation software and services.
- How much of that spend is tied to wind or weather sensitive routing vs general OPS software.

---

## 2. SAM (Serviceable Available Market)

**Definition for this opportunity**
Airlines that fly long haul with large wind exposure, buy third party planning or weather tools, and could adopt a verification first fuel path product in the next few years. Focus geography and ops style that match Emirates class networks (hub long haul, widebody heavy).

**What we know now**
- Long haul hub carriers feel wind and ISA effects more than short hop networks.
- Middle East long haul is a natural first beachhead for a Dubai rooted story.

**What research must fill**
- Count of long haul carriers and approximate software budget band for flight planning / fuel tools.
- Which regions buy new vendors vs lock into Lido, NAVBLUE, Jeppesen, Sabre stacks.

---

## 3. SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)

**Definition for this opportunity**
What we can realistically win in 3 to 5 years: Emirates first pilot, then a small set of similar long haul carriers, without claiming network wide airline savings on day one.

**What we know now**
- v1 scope is Emirates Dubai long haul (OMDB origin city pairs).
- Product is advisory: human keeps final say; not a certified flight plan replacer.

**What research must fill**
- Realistic first year revenue if Emirates (or one peer) buys a pilot then a paid seat.
- Expansion path: other Gulf carriers, European long haul, Asian long haul.

---

## 4. TECHNICAL COMPLEXITY

**Definition for this opportunity**
How hard it is to build, prove, and operate a system that (a) verifies weather on flown tracks and (b) only then ranks paths by trip fuel in kilograms.

### Nested technical rules (weather gate and fuel)

These are the hard product rules under this commercial dimension.

**Primary technical goal**
Trip fuel in kilograms. Not quantum theatre. Not auto filed plans.

**Two phases**
1. Phase 1: forecast vs reality on real flown paths (ADS-B or QAR). Suitability gate.
2. Phase 2: only if gate passes, rank nearby paths and flight levels by corrected wind fuel.

**Must pass technical checks**

| ID | Rule | How you check |
|----|------|---------------|
| T1 | Primary goal is trip fuel in kilograms | Written in the pilot brief before work starts |
| T2 | Scope is Dubai long haul city pairs for v1 | Origin OMDB; long haul destinations only |
| T3 | Uses real flown paths | ADS-B or QAR track exists for each test flight |
| T4 | Forecast vs reality check runs first | Phase 1 report exists before Phase 2 |
| T5 | Weather gate must pass | Suitability ≥ 0.55 and n ≥ 6 on wind or temp |
| T6 | Win number written first | Beat no wind plan by X kg on Y flights (fill before run) |
| T7 | Human keeps final say | Ranked suggestions only |
| T8 | Every claim cites evidence | flight id, weather source, sample count, date |

**Strong technical pilot extras**
Held out test days; compare more than one weather product; report forecast age; show along track weather changes; prefer strong wind days; keep MOS only if error shrinks.

**Hard fails**
- Rank paths without a weather gate
- Use only planned map routes, no flown track
- Score a weather source against itself as truth
- Change the win number after seeing results
- Claim it replaces certified flight plans
- Claim quantum advantage is proven
- Promise network wide airline savings from one pilot
- Sell short haul calm routes as main proof

**Technical success scorecard**
- PASS: all T1 to T8 true, and win number hit on the agreed flight set
- CONDITIONAL PASS: T1 to T8 true, win number missed, weather gate works, next fix listed
- FAIL: any must pass false, or rankings only work with perfect hindsight

**What we know now**
- Weather forecast uncertainty is the biggest hard problem.
- Code path exists: Phase 1 verify, Phase 2 rank, along track timeline, free extracts.
- Core finding from prior study: exact math can look good with fresh weather and fail with old weather.

**What research must fill**
- How incumbents handle forecast trust (or if they mostly assume weather is good enough).
- What data Emirates already has (QAR, preferred weather vendors, ops constraints).
- Effort to reach airline grade reliability under granted levels and ATC reality.

## 5. COMPLIANCE

**Definition for this opportunity**
Rules, certifications, and airline IT barriers that block or slow a non certified advisory tool sitting beside dispatch and operations control.

**What we know now**
- Tool must not claim to replace certified flight plans.
- Human authority stays with airline ops.
- Aviation data has vendor contracts, privacy, and export style limits.

**What research must fill**
- What Emirates / GCAA / ops manuals require for advisory fuel tools.
- Data room barriers: QAR access, ADS-B policy, weather license reuse.
- Cyber and vendor onboarding steps typical for Emirates class carriers.

---

## 6. SALES CYCLE

**Definition for this opportunity**
Time and stages from first meeting to paid pilot to production use at Emirates (and peers).

**What we know now**
- Airline software sales are multi stakeholder (ops, fuel, IT, safety).
- A forecast verification first story needs a technical champion who cares about fuel kg evidence.

**What research must fill**
- Typical cycle length for flight planning / fuel tools at Emirates class airlines.
- Who signs (fuel savings owner vs flight ops vs digital).
- What proof pack closes a pilot (held out kg, weather gate charts, risk limits).

---

## 7. HOW TO REACH

**Definition for this opportunity**
Concrete paths to decision makers and champions: partners, conferences, warm intros, OEM or weather vendor channels, Qorina style PoC relationships.

**What we know now**
- Prior materials and Qorina style work exist as conversation starters.
- Product is strongest when shown on real EK flown tracks, not slides alone.

**What research must fill**
- Named entry points for Emirates fuel / ops / digital teams.
- Whether Lido / NAVBLUE / Jeppesen / weather providers partner or block.
- Best first meeting offer (weather gate demo on EK city pairs).

---

## 8. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

**Definition for this opportunity**
What Emirates (and peers) will pay for verified fuel path advice: pilot fee, SaaS seat, per flight, or shared savings.

**What we know now**
- Fuel savings in kilograms have clear cash value when proven.
- Airlines already pay large vendors for planning suites, so budget lines exist.

**What research must fill**
- Price bands for OptiFlight class tools and weather uplift products.
- Preference for shared savings vs fixed SaaS.
- What kg proof level unlocks budget vs what stays as R&D curiosity.

---

## 9. PROBLEM COST

**Definition for this opportunity**
How much money (and ops pain) wrong wind and weak path choice cost Emirates today on Dubai long haul, including fuel burn, contingency fuel culture, and missed wind opportunities.

**What we know now**
- Wind and ISA drive large fuel swings on long haul.
- Bad or old forecasts can erase or reverse a clever path choice.
- Problem cost is the commercial why behind the weather gate.

**What research must fill**
- Published or credible ranges for fuel waste from suboptimal wind routing.
- Emirates specific or peer published fuel efficiency programs and savings claims.
- Cost of contingency fuel and dispatch conservatism tied to weather distrust.

---

## How to use this model

1. Score each of the 9 dimensions as Known / Partial / Unknown after every research pass.
2. Do not claim commercial PASS until PROBLEM COST, WILLINGNESS TO PAY, and SOM have numbers, not vibes.
3. Do not claim technical PASS until nested T1 to T8 and the win number rules hold.
4. Market research answers dimensions 1 to 3 and 5 to 9. Flight data work answers the nested technical section of dimension 4.
